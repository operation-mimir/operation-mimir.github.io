*If $f$ is a bounded function on $[a,b]$, then $L(f) \leq U(f)$.*
## Proof
[[ross elem analysis/32/lem 32.3|lem 32.3]] shows that for arbitrary partitions $P$ and $Q$ of $[a,b]$, 

$$
L(f,P) \leq U(f,Q)
$$

Since $Q$ is arbitrary, $L(f,P)$ is a lower bound for $L(f,P)$. Then the relation between $L(f,P)$, a lower bound, and $U(f)$, the infimum, is

$$
L(f,P) \leq U(f)
$$

Once again, $P$ is arbitrary, so $U(f)$ is an upper bound of $L(f,P)$, so the relation between $U(P)$ and $L(f)$ is

$$
L(f) \leq U(f) \tag*{$\blacksquare$}
$$
