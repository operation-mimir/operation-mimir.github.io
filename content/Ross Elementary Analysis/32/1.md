## Find the upper and lower Darboux integrals for $f(x)=x^3$ on the interval $[0,b]$. Hint: [[Ross Elementary Analysis/1/3|Exercise 1.3]] and [[Ross Elementary Analysis/1/Example 1|Example 1]] in [[Ross Elementary Analysis/1/index|§1]] will be useful.
$f$ is defined by $f(x) = x^3$ so $f$ is increasing on $[0,b]$, $M(f,[t_{k-1},t_k])=f(t_k)=t_k^3$ and $m(f,[t_{k-1},t_k])=f(t_{k-1})=t_{k-1}^3$.

Using this fact and the [[Ross Elementary Analysis/32/Definition 32.1|Definition 32.1]]

$$
U(f,P)=\sum_{k=1}^n t_k^3(t_k-t_{k-1})
$$

$$
L(f,P)=\sum_{k=1}^n t_{k-1}^3(t_k-t_{k-1})
$$

If we choose the partition $P$ such that $t_k=\frac{kb}{n}$, then

$$
U(f,P)=\sum_{k=1}^n \frac{k^3b^3}{n^3}\left(\frac{b}{n}\right)=\frac{b^4}{n^4}\sum_{k=1}^n k^3 = \frac{b^4}{n^4}\cdot \left(\frac{n(n+1)}{2}\right)^2
$$
$$
L(f,P)=\sum_{k=1}^n \frac{(k-1)^3b^3}{n^3}\left(\frac{b}{n}\right)=\frac{b^4}{n^4}\sum_{k=1}^n (k-1)^3 = \frac{b^4}{n^4}\cdot \left(\frac{(n-1)n}{2}\right)^2
$$

Thus we get $U(f,P)>\frac{b^4}{4}$ and $L(f,P)<\frac{b^4}{4}$. Thus the infimum of $U(f,P)$, the upper Darboux integral $U(f) \leq \frac{b^4}{4}$ and supremum of $L(f,P)$, the lower Darboux integral $L(f)\geq\frac{b^4}{4}$.

This combined with [[Ross Elementary Analysis/32/Theorem 32.4|Theorem 32.4]] gives
$$
U(f) = L(f) = \frac{b^4}{4}
$$
