## Let $f(x)=x$ for rational $x$ and $f(x)=0$ for irrational $x$. <br> (a) Calculate the upper and lower Darboux integrals for $f$ on the interval $[0,b]$. <br> (b) Is $f$ integrable on $[0,b]$?

Let $P=\{0=t_0<t_1<\cdots<t_n=b\}$ be a partition of $[0,b]$.

In any interval $[t_{k-1},t_k]$ for $k\in\{1,2,\ldots n\}$, $M(f,[t_{k-1},t_k])=t_k$ and $m(f,[t_{k-1},t_k])=0$.

Thus by [[ross analysis/32/def 1|Definition 32.1]]

$$
U(f,P)=\sum_{k=1}^n t_k(t_k-t_{k-1})
$$

and

$$
L(f,P)=0
$$

If we choose the partition $P$ such that $t_k=\frac{kb}{n}$, then

$$
U(f,P)=\sum_{k=1}^n \frac{kb}{n}\cdot\left(\frac{b}{n}\right)=\left(\frac{b}{n}\right)^2\sum_{k=1}^n k=\frac{b^2}{n^2}\cdot\frac{n(n+1)}{2}
$$

Now $U(f,P)>\frac{b^2}{2}$ so $U(f) \geq \frac{b^2}{2}$.

Also

$$
U(f,P)\geq\left(\frac{b}{n}\right)^2\sum_{k=1}^n (k-1) = \frac{b^2}{n^2}\cdot\frac{n(n-1)}{2}=\frac{b^2}{2}\left(1-\frac{1}{n}\right)
$$

Now $U(f,P) \geq \frac{b^2}{2}\left(1-\frac{1}{n}\right)$ so $U(f) \geq \frac{b^2}{2}\left(1-\frac{1}{n}\right)$.

Rearranging the terms

$$
\frac{b^2}{2} - U(f) \leq \frac{b^2}{2}\left(\frac{1}{n}\right)
$$

But $U(f) - \frac{b^2}{2} \geq 0$ since $U(f) \geq \frac{b^2}{2}$.

Thus $U(f) = \frac{b^2}{2}$.

So $U(f)\neq L(f)$ and $f$ is not integrable.
