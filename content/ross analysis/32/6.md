## Let $f$ be a bounded function on $[a,b]$. Suppose there exist sequences $(U_n)$ and $(L_n)$ of upper and lower Darboux sums for $f$ such that $\lim{(U_n-L_n)}=0$. Show $f$ is integrable and $\int^a_bf=\lim{U_n}=\lim{L_n}$.

$$
\begin{align}
U(f) \leq U_n \qquad \forall n\in \mathbb{N} \\
L(f) \geq L_N \qquad \forall n \in \mathbb{N}
\end{align}
$$

So

$$
0 \leq U(f) - L(f) \leq U_n - L_n \qquad \forall n \in \mathbb{N}
$$

Since $\lim{(U_n - L_n)} = 0$, using Squeeze Theorem, $\lim{( U(f) - L(f)} = 0$

As this is a constant sequence, $U(f) = L(f) = \int_a^b f$.

So $f$ is integrable on $[a,b]$.

Let $\epsilon > 0$ be given, since $\lim{( U(f) - L(f)} = 0$, there exists a $N \in \mathbb{N}$ such that for all $n > N$

$$
|U_n - L_n| < \epsilon 
$$

Now we know that $L_n \leq U_n$ and $|U_n - L_n| = U_n - L_n$

So 

$$
U _n - L_n < \epsilon \qquad \forall n > N
$$

Consider

$$
\left|U_n - \int_a^b f \right| = U_n - \int_a^b f \leq U_n - L_n < \epsilon \qquad \forall n > N
$$

Thus $\lim{U_n} = \int_a^b f$ and similarly $\lim{L_n} = \int_a^b f$.
